## The gap, as a rate
{{kg to lose}} over {{weeks}} ≈ **{{kg/week}}**. Name the real risk: for most trained
people it is losing *too fast* from too little food and giving away muscle — not losing too slowly.

## Core principles (ordered by impact)
1. **Progressive resistance training, heavy, ≥2×/week** — the signal that tells muscle to stay is load.
2. **Protein ≥1.6 g/kg/day, spread across 3–4 feedings** — total and distribution both matter.
3. **Set a calorie floor, not a ceiling** — a deficit you didn't intend is where muscle goes.
4. **Drop sets, never drop the weight** — when energy is low, cut volume and keep intensity.
5. **NEAT before extra cardio** — walking costs no recovery.

## Diagnostics — measure these, not just the scale
- Working loads on 3–4 key lifts at the same RPE (earliest muscle-loss signal).
- Waist at the navel, weekly, same conditions.
- Grip strength monthly.
- Practical check: >0.8 kg lost in a week twice in a row → eat more, not less.

## Plateau troubleshooting — in this order
1. Is it real? A stall under 10–14 days is noise. Use the 14-day average.
2. Has intake crept up? Track honestly for 5 days before changing anything.
3. Has daily movement dropped? Check steps against a month ago.
4. Is protein still on target?
5. Only then change the plan — and never cut further while already at the floor.

## Escalation
Dizziness, fainting, chest symptoms, persistent GI issues, loads dropping across multiple
lifts for 3+ weeks, or weight loss consistently above ~1% of body weight/week → doctor.
